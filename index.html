About Me

Software Engineer who builds at the intersection of high-scale systems and high-stakes data. I specialize in making sure that when 'real-world' data hits production, the system doesn't just survive, it performs.

At Philips, I led the development of the Patient Administration Toolkit, engineering interoperability workflows that managed 10,000+ daily transactions. By optimizing these pipelines, I reduced data errors by 35% and significantly cut patient processing times. I’ve also spent time in R&D, building NLP models for MRI complaint categorization that achieved 87% accuracy in production.

I try to treat system design like an insurance policy: you hope you never need the edge-case handling, but you're glad it's there when the data gets messy.

The Question I'm Chasing: We spend so much time teaching models how to be right but are we spending enough time teaching them how to signal when they’re about to be wrong? If you’re interested in building models that knows its own limits, let’s talk.
